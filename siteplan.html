Site Plan

Lagos Street Food Guide  ·  Individual Website Project

1 · Site Name

Lagos Street Food Guide

Proposed domain: lagosstreetfood.com

The name directly communicates the site's subject and geography. Lagos is one of Africa's most vibrant culinary cities, and its street-food culture is globally underrepresented online. "Guide" signals that the site is a curated resource — not just a listing — helping users make confident decisions about what to eat and where.

2 · Site Purpose

What the Site Does

Lagos Street Food Guide is a three-page informational site that celebrates and documents the city's iconic street foods. It will provide:

  • A home page featuring a hero introduction, a dynamically generated grid of 15+ dishes (loaded from a local JSON file), and a spotlight on the dish of the week.
  • A vendors & spots page listing recommended street-food locations with ratings, neighbourhood tags, and a modal for full details.
  • A culture & tips page covering the history of Lagos street food, eating etiquette, and a visitor contact/feedback form.

JavaScript will power data fetching, dynamic rendering, modal dialogs, and local-storage–based user preferences (e.g., saved favourite dishes).

3 · Scenarios

Target Audience Questions

"I'm visiting Lagos for the first time — which street foods should I absolutely try, and where can I find them safely?"
"I grew up eating suya and pepper soup — are there any vendors near Lagos Island that are considered the most authentic?"
"What's the difference between boli and roasted plantain, and how do locals typically eat them?"
4 · Color Scheme

Palette

Pepper Red — #C0392B Primary brand color. Header background, headings, section borders, CTAs.
Suya Orange — #E67E22 Secondary accent. Card borders, hover states, highlights.
Warm Cream — #FDF6EC Page background. Keeps the feel warm and food-friendly.
Dark Espresso — #1A1209 Body text, footer text.
Plantain Green — #27AE60 Accent for success states, tags, and blockquote borders.
5 · Typography

Font Selections

Playfair Display

Usage: Page headings (h1, h2), logo wordmark, dish names on cards — brings editorial warmth and Nigerian print-culture references.

DM Sans — Body copy, navigation links, labels, captions, form fields, and all utility text. Clear and highly readable at small sizes.

Usage: All body text, UI elements, form fields, captions.

Both fonts are loaded from Google Fonts. A maximum of two font families keeps page weight low and maintains visual consistency across all three pages.
